## Survivor triage

**G4 -- killable, and pointing at a real gap.** Git porcelain reports a staged rename as `R  old -> new`. The path `collect` would write is the one on the *right*; recording the left one leaves the renamed file unguarded. No test exercised a rename, so the mutant lived. This matters more than its narrowness suggests: reorganising a payload -- cutting content into `machines/<box>/`, which is exactly what a first-contact adoption involves -- is mostly renames, and those are precisely the hours of uncommitted work #13 exists to protect.

Closed by `test_a_staged_rename_marks_the_NEW_path_dirty` in `tests/test_uncommitted_checkout_guard.py`. Round 2 (spec `v058-spec-round2.json`, report `v058-report-round2.json`): `{"killed": 1}`.

**G3 -- equivalent.** See `equivalents.md`. Recorded rather than closed, because no test can distinguish it.

## Note on method

Both survivors were reached by triage, not by re-running with more mutants. The G3/G4 split is the useful illustration: the two mutants sat on adjacent lines of the same small function and looked equally like coverage gaps, but one was unkillable by construction and the other was a genuine defect in a case nobody had thought about. Counting survivors would have scored them the same; reading them did not.
